## 1 · 🔴 The correction: the €1 floor was the pacer, not the rules

The tab study's headline — *"58 of 86 live campaigns now sit at €1 because of [the two AUTO
rules]"* — is **wrong for 56 of the 58**, and had been carried into four further documents and into
the page's own warning banner.

Measured with `_bud-page-split816.mts`: for each at-floor campaign, the **transition write** — the
row taking it from >€1 to ≤€1 — classified by author and shape.

| cause | n | now/day | pre-floor/day | signature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`automation:budget-manager-cron`** | **56** | €56.00 | **€1,276.00** | one write, straight to €1 |
| the two AUTO rules | **2** | €2.00 | €2.16 | ≥3 rule cuts in the 72h before |
| neither | 0 | — | — | |

**55 of the 58 were floored inside a single hour** — 2026-08-05, 02:00–03:00 — carrying €1,261/day
between them, in writes shaped `€100 → €1`, `€60 → €1`, `€20 → €1`. The two real ratchet victims,
`GALE EXACT IT` (€1.10 → €1) and `GALE EXACT DE` (€1.06 → €1), were **already at ~€1 when the rules
reached them**.

### What fired, in the engine's own words

`ads-budget-enforce.service.ts:103` documents the incident in a comment above the fix:

> pacing was *prescriptive*, rewriting every daily budget to the pacing target regardless of cap
> risk. On 2026-08-05 the account was **€367 into a €4,000 month — 91% under, projecting ~€2,850 —
> and pacing still proposed cutting 82 campaigns by €1,826/day.**

Target is `todayTarget × (campMtd ÷ spendTotal)`, so a campaign with near-zero month-to-date spend
gets a near-zero target, clamped to `FLOOR_CENTS` = €1. That is the €100 → €1.

**Can it fire again unchanged? No.** `pacingNeeded = autoPacing && cap > 0 && projected > cap`
(service:114) now gates it. But the engine is live and armed: `NEXUS_BUDGET_ENFORCE_APPLY=1`, all
four plans `autoPacing=ON stopOverSpend=ON`, every 30 minutes.

### Why the wrong version was plausible

Every measurement about the ratchet was true — the compounding, the dead cap, the repeat-write loop,
the €4.42 → €1.00 sequence. None of it was the *cause*. The question never asked was **which write
put each campaign on the floor**. A compounding sequence leaves 39 audit rows and tells a story; a
single `€100 → €1` leaves one row and looks like a setting. The loud mechanism took the credit for
the quiet one's damage.

## 2 · 🔴 The floor is built at the wrong layer

§3.1 of the brief calls setting `minBudgetCents` "the single highest-value act available". As built
it **cannot stop a local cut**.

`minBudgetCents` has exactly one reader: `ads-write-gate.ts:349`. That gate runs in
`ads-sync.worker.ts`, at **dispatch**. `updateCampaignWithSync` does `prisma.campaign.update` first
and there is **no `checkAdsWriteGate` anywhere in `ads-mutation.service.ts`**. So a floor stops the
cut *reaching Amazon* — the exact mechanism BUD.1 §1.2 documented for `liveBidWritesEnabled`, and
which BUD.2's own gate comment cites while placing the floor in the same position.

**The experiment has already run.** The four MOSS campaigns absorbed **488 denied cuts — 122 each** —
local value returning to €10 via resync every time. They *are* protected on Amazon, through a
permanent repeat-write loop. Setting this floor on 56 IT campaigns would manufacture 56 more.

**Not armed. Recommendation: move the check to before `prisma.campaign.update` — in
`updateCampaignWithSync`, or in the two handlers — then set values.** The derivation is ready and is
recorded below, so arming it afterwards is a data change, not an analysis.

### The derivation, ready to use

Naively — "the smallest budget that has delivered a click" — returns **€1.00** for IT, DE and FR,
because the floored campaigns clicked *before* they were floored. Excluding pacer-floored campaigns:

| market | floor | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| IT | **€1.51** | IT-AIREON-SP-Category-Broad — 60 clicks, €24.54 |
| DE | **€8.87** | GALE BROAD DE — 362 clicks, €243.53 |
| ES | **€6.38** | ES_Phrase_3_Keywords — 450 clicks, €242.39 |
| FR | **€3.09** | FR_Phrase_8_Keywords — 255 clicks, €119.67 |

60-day replay: **650 writes denied across 68 campaigns — 56 of them the 2026-08-05 sweep itself.**
Not a floor that would have refused nothing.

## 3 · 🔴 A restore would not survive — and baselines are the same act

`ad-budget-enforce` runs every 30 minutes. It does not fire today (IT 10% under, DE 42%, ES 9%,
FR 12%). **Restoring makes it fire.**

| market | at floor | wants/day | envelope/day | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **IT** | 55 | **€1,252.10** | **€12.62** | 🔴 1% of the ask fits |
| ES | 1 | €10.00 | €0.66 | 🔴 7% |
| FR | 1 | €15.00 | €0.54 | 🔴 4% |
| DE | 1 | €1.06 | €140.47 | 🟢 fits |

A restored ceiling lets spend rise; spend raises `projected`; when the predicate flips, targets are
computed by **spend share** — and a just-restored campaign has ten days of €1-constrained spend, so
its share is ~0 and it is clamped straight back to the floor. **2026-08-05 again, executed by the
corrected engine.**

**§3.2 is not separable from §3.4.** A baseline above the current value makes the next rule tick
*raise* the campaign (−20% of a €3 baseline is €2.40 against €1 now). The two AUTO rules would
execute the restore for us. Baselines for the 58 are a restore by another route.

**The plan arithmetic:** €2,220 + €1,445 + €210 + €125 = €4,000/month = **€129.03/day**. The
pre-floor ask is **€1,278.16/day — 9.9× the entire plan.** (Pre-floor, not the €1,476 peak: the peak
is a campaign-creation default, not an operator decision.)

**Neither armed.** Recovery is a monthly-plan decision — tab 4's `AdBudgetPlan` — not a tab 6 act.

That ends the instant anything is restored. **Recommendation: leave autonomy unchanged, and make
`PROPOSE` a precondition of any restore rather than a standalone act.** Unchanged in this session.
