## 1 · 🔴 Four corrections to the study

### 1.1 · The 488 "PENDING" rows are 488 gate-DENIED writes, and the loop cannot converge

The study read them as a settling delay:

> *488 of 2,386 rows are `PENDING` — the write is queued to Amazon but the local `Campaign.dailyBudget`
> still reads the old value, so the next tick recomputes the same cut and re-issues it.*

Measured (`_bud-page-gate.mts`): `OutboundSyncQueue` for `syncType = 'AD_BUDGET_UPDATE'` holds
**1,898 SUCCESS and 488 SKIPPED, and all 488 SKIPPED are `WRITE_GATE_DENIED`.** They belong to
exactly four campaigns — 122 each, 4 × 122 = 488:

```
MOSS-Competitor-SP-KW-TM   €10.00 → €1.00   amz=PENDING   queue=SKIPPED/WRITE_GATE_DENIED   ×122
MOSS-Generic-SP-KW-TM      €10.00 → €1.00   amz=PENDING   queue=SKIPPED/WRITE_GATE_DENIED   ×122
MOSS-Brand-SP-KW-TM        …×122        MOSS-Auto-SP-KW-TM   …×122
```

Every one is `€10.00 → €1.00`, every 30 minutes, by `budget-manager-cron`. `payloadBefore` is
€10.00 on all 122, and the live value is **still €10.00** — so the local write does not stick
either. This is not a value waiting to settle: it is a **closed loop**. The gate guarantees Amazon
never accepts the new number, something restores the local one before the next tick, and the same
cut is recomputed forever. It ran 488 times and stopped only when the pacer went quiet.

**`amazonResponseStatus` is stamped `PENDING` at enqueue and never corrected when the worker skips
the row.** It cannot answer "did this reach Amazon". `OutboundSyncQueue.syncStatus` can, and the
service reads that instead.

### 1.2 · The write gate does not protect a campaign from a cut — it makes it diverge

`updateCampaignWithSync` (`ads-mutation.service.ts:591`) writes the local `Campaign` row with **no
gate call**; `checkAdsWriteGate` runs later, in `ads-sync.worker.ts:356`, at dispatch. `grep` finds
zero occurrences of `checkAdsWriteGate` in the mutation service.

So the study's *"campaigns a trim rule can still move: 24"* is the count that reaches **Amazon**.
The count that gets **cut** is **28**. The page therefore carries two census numbers, not one, and
never describes the 4 as protected — they are the only four campaigns in the account whose local
budget is knowingly allowed to disagree with Amazon's.

### 1.3 · The ratchet is armed, not firing — and both one-word readings are wrong

`AD_BUDGET_UPDATE` per day (`_bud-page-tempo.mts`):

| day | 08-05 | 08-06 | 08-07 | 08-08 | 08-09 | 08-10 | 08-11 | 08-12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| writes | 234 | 476 | **727** | 564 | 354 | **11** | **1** | **0** |

Meanwhile the rules keep executing: 08-11 was 480 DRY_RUN + 96 SUCCESS, and today 10 + 5. **58 of
86 campaigns are pinned at €1 and there is nothing left to cut.** The brief's "🔴 the ratchet is
LIVE" overstates the last 48 hours; "it stopped" would badly understate the danger. The page says
the accurate thing: *armed, matching, and out of target space — the first budget raised gets eaten
again.*

### 1.4 · Two of the six rules' levels differ between the stored dial and the effective one

`resolveAutonomy` (`enabled=false` ⇒ OFF regardless of the dial):

| rule | study's column | effective |
|---|---|---|
| Trim budget on weak ACOS | AUTO | **AUTO** |
| Campaign ACOS rebalance (cut + scale) | AUTO | **AUTO** |
| Scale budget-capped winners | PROPOSE | PROPOSE |
| Boost budget on profitable campaigns | PROPOSE | PROPOSE |
| Trim budget on weak ACOS *(duplicate)* | PROPOSE | **OFF** |
| Scale budget on ROAS winners | OFF | OFF |

Which is exactly why the brief demanded `resolveAutonomy` rather than the stored column.

**Everything else in the study verified unchanged**, including 86 ENABLED · 58 at floor · €318.57/day
total · all six account-scoped · both AUTO rules cut-only · `Boost budget on profitable campaigns`
at 0 matches in 4,864 evaluations.

## 2 · The cap, re-measured — and why the page states it where it does

The study's finding held, and today's numbers are sharper:

| rule | cap | production predicate | correct predicate | rows today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scale budget-capped winners | 10 | **0** | 10 | 10 |
| Campaign ACOS rebalance | 5 | **0** | 5 | 5 |

`NOT (errorMessage = 'DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED')` is NULL — not TRUE — for the null `errorMessage` every
SUCCESS and DRY_RUN row carries, so the count excludes every successful execution.
**`Campaign ACOS rebalance` has a cap of 5, has run 5 times today, and has not been refused.**

`FAILED` executions per day stop dead after **2026-08-03** — 180, 83, 182, 75, 166 on the days
before, zero on every day since. That is the date the clause landed.

The page renders this warning **next to the "Refused" column**, because a column of zeroes there
otherwise reads as reassurance when it means the opposite: nothing *can* be refused.

**Not fixed, per the brief.** The predicate is shared by all eleven tabs' rules and repairing it
would instantly re-arm refusals account-wide before the caps have been re-sized.

## 3 · The cursor — where Bid's design does not transfer

Bid's load-bearing field is `max(AdTarget.updatedAt)`, because an inbound resync moves a bid and
writes no audit row. The mirror-image question here gets the opposite answer.

| candidate | last 24 h |
|---|---|
| `Campaign.updatedAt` | fires in **7 distinct minutes** (219 rows; one burst of 200 at 01:00–01:02 — the settings resync) |
| `AD_BUDGET_UPDATE` | **6 writes in 48 h** |

A cursor on `Campaign.updatedAt` would raise "changed" more often wrongly than rightly, and a banner
that is usually wrong is one the operator learns to ignore. There is no `budgetUpdatedAt` column.

**So the fingerprint is the value**: `budgetCents` = Σ `Campaign.dailyBudget` over the scope.

```ts
view=campaigns → { view, budgetCents, n, loggedAt }
view=rules     → { view, execAt, execN, loggedAt }
```

- `budgetCents` moves iff a budget in scope moves — **including a value restored with no audit row**,
  which is precisely the MOSS mechanism `loggedAt` alone cannot see.
- `loggedAt` covers what the sum cannot: a compensating pair inside one poll window.
- **View-shaped** because the two views render different numbers; a rules cursor watching the sum
  would never move, a campaigns cursor watching executions would move every 15 minutes over numbers
  that view does not show. Payload and poll are built by the same function from the same `view`, so
  key sets always match and `useCursorPoll`'s structural equality needed no change.

**Proven both ways, because stability alone is not a proof — a cursor that never moves is stable too.**

*It does not fire when nothing happens*: sampled twice 60 s apart on prod, every field identical,
0 campaigns touched, 0 writes.

*It fires when something does*: the ratchet is dormant right now (0 writes today, 1 in 48 h), so
waiting for a live write would have proven nothing. Replayed instead — for every real
`AD_BUDGET_UPDATE` in 14 days, reconstruct the scope sum before and after and check the cursor
differs:

| | |
|---|---|
| real writes in scope, 14 days | **2,381** |
| ...that would have moved `budgetCents` | **2,381** |
| ...with a zero delta (invisible) | **0** |
| ...with an unreadable payload | **0** |

End to end on the most recent: `FR_Phrase_8_Keywords €3.11 → €3.09` by `budget-manager-cron` moves
the scope sum **€318.59 → €318.57 (−€0.02)** — and €318.57 is the live value the page renders today,
so the replay closes against reality rather than against itself. The cursor also agrees with an
independent sum over the 86 ENABLED campaigns.

`loggedAt` is belt-and-braces here and is reported as such: **0 minutes in this window held writes
whose deltas cancel**, so the sum alone would have been sufficient for all 2,381 — but the field
costs one indexed read and covers the case that would otherwise be silent.

Not SSE: that bus carries 0.21% of writes and is blind to `budget-manager-cron`, which made 1,164
of the 2,386 changes.

## 4 · Two defects the pre-deploy smoke test caught

`_bud-page-verify.mts` calls the service the way the route does, before the route existed.

1. **The cursor described a different row set from the grid.** It summed all 220 campaigns
   (€8,768.57) while the page showed the 86 ENABLED (€318.57), and would have gone stale on a PAUSED
   campaign moving. `status` now reaches the cursor, and the cursor resolves the exact ids the grid
   renders. This is Bid's first rule — *the cursor must describe the same row set the page is
   showing* — and it was broken by omission rather than by disagreement.
2. **`portfolio` + `campaign` together resolved to zero.** The exclusivity rule lived only in the
   route handler, so the grid and the cursor could resolve different scopes from one query string.
   It now lives in the service's `resolveScope`, which both call.

Neither was visible to `tsc`.

### And three the deployed page caught that the code review did not

Measured, not read: the census **wrapped to two rows at 1280** because I put the three-up breakpoint
at 1400 on an assumption (the content box is 1154px there, so six cells are 186px and every label
fits); the scope captions **stacked above their selects**, making the bar 72px; and the first draft
stacked **three warning blocks, 225px of them**, above the grid. Together the first two put scope +
census at 198px against a 120px budget. Fixed in `5233c750c`: 113px, one census row, one warning.

The three warnings became one because the ratchet and the dead cap are one story. The write-gate
divergence was not deleted — it moved to where it can be acted on: the *"a trim can still cut"*
census cell states the 28 / 24 / 4 split in its tooltip, and the **Reaches Amazon** column reads
*"cut only locally"* on each of the four rows it describes. A finding attached to the row it
describes beats a banner above a grid.

### 🔴 And one process failure worth recording, because this document already warns about it

The one-line contrast fix, committed with `git commit --only`, **swept 132 lines of PLC, AR, SOV and
KT CSS into it** — `--only` takes the *working tree* version of a path, and four sessions had
appended to the shared stylesheet since my previous commit. That is locks §5 exactly, in the
direction the document has now recorded six times.

What let it through was not ignorance of the rule but the *shape* of the check: I ran
`git diff -U0 | grep '^@@'` in the **same command** as the commit, so it printed the two hunks and
committed anyway. **A check has to gate the commit, not accompany it.** Undone with `reset --soft`
before it left the machine, then rebuilt as index surgery — HEAD plus my five lines, asserted to
carry exactly one hunk and only the `h10-bud-` prefix, with the working tree restored to all four
sessions' work afterwards, and the assertion wired as `&&` so a failure could not reach `git commit`.
A second lesson underneath it: **the git index is shared between sessions too** — a `git add` of mine
was cleared by a concurrent session between two of my commands, which is why the final version
stages, verifies and commits as one atomic step.

## 7 · What BUD.2 inherits

- **The ratchet is untouched and still armed.** Two AUTO rules, −15% and −20% of the *current*
  value, every 15 minutes, no cooldown, no floor above Amazon's €1, no baseline, and no working cap.
- **`liveBidWritesEnabled` is not a brake** — see §1.2. A guardrail that relies on it will not hold.
- **`OutboundSyncQueue.syncStatus`, never `amazonResponseStatus`**, for "did this reach Amazon".
- **The repeat-write loop needs the in-flight write treated as the current value**, or it will
  re-arm the moment a budget is raised above €1.
- **`AdvertisingActionLog` budgets are euros.** Every field crossing the page's seam is `…Cents`
  and an integer; the conversion happens once, on the server.
- Six section shells are named in `BudgetSections.tsx` and the slot props are already flowing.
  BUD.2 is one new file and one import line — and it is the first section allowed to replace
  `NO_WRITE_ACTIONS`, which it must replace rather than quietly stop passing.
