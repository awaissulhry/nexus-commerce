# P4.4c — the operator's pricing floor and ceiling, honoured at the send

**Date** 2026-09-21 · **Package** P4.4 (Price), third slice · **Size** M

The plan row: *"Min/max guard on every price write."*

---

## 1. What was measured

### 1.1 The floor exists, is settable, is displayed — and binds nothing

`Product.minPrice` ("Pricing floor") and `Product.maxPrice` ("Pricing ceiling")
are columns in the pricing-intelligence block. **Nine screens read them**
(`products.routes`, `products-catalog.routes`, `matrix.routes`, `inventory`,
`listing-wizard.routes`, …), `bulk-upload.service` writes them, and an operator
can set them from the products grid.

**No price write consulted either of them.** Measured across the seven paths
that push a price:

| File | `minPrice` / `maxPrice` occurrences |
|---|---|
| `services/outbound-sync.service.ts` | **0** |
| `services/master-price.service.ts` | **0** |
| `services/pim/channel-price-write.service.ts` | **0** |
| `services/shopify/listing-write.service.ts` | **0** |
| `services/ebay-variation-push.service.ts` | **0** |
| `services/amazon/flat-file.service.ts` | **0** |
| `routes/catalog.routes.ts` | **0** |
| `services/repricing.service.ts` | 16 ← **positive control** |

The zero is *measured empty*, not *could not measure*: the same search finds 16
in the repricer.

🔴 **And the repricer's 16 are a DIFFERENT PAIR.** They are
`RepricingRule.minPrice/maxPrice` — a per-rule range whose model comment says
*"Floor/ceiling are HARD constraints — every strategy clamps its computed price
to [minPrice, maxPrice]"*. Two fields with the same names on two models, one
binding a machine lane and one binding nothing.

So an operator who sets a floor of €85 on a product sees it on five screens, and
a typed price of €79.99, a master-price cascade, a flat-file save, a wizard
publish and a bulk action all go straight past it.

### 1.2 The shape, again

This is P4.3d's shape in the price lane: **a guard that exists on one lane and
not at the door.** It is also the P2.x shape — a field referenced by working code
around it that has never once bound a send.

## 2. What was built, and the two decisions inside it

`services/price-bounds.service.ts`: a pure verdict (`priceBoundsRefusal`), a
loader (`loadPriceBounds`) and the one call a lane makes (`priceRefusalFor`).
Wired into the **Amazon, eBay and Shopify** lanes, beside the quantity guards.

### 2.1 🔴 It REFUSES rather than clamping — deliberately unlike its neighbour

The oversell clamp two lines away silently reduces a quantity. This does not
silently change a price, and the difference is the point:

> **A quantity clamp reduces a number the system derived. A price outside the
> operator's own floor is a number a PERSON typed, and quietly sending a
> different one is worse than not sending it.**

The repricer may still clamp inside its own rule — a machine choosing within a
range it was given is a different act from overriding a typed number.

### 2.2 🔴 It fails OPEN, deliberately unlike D9

P4.3b made the EU quantity guard fail **closed**, and four fail-opens were fixed
across this package, so the reflex by now is "refuse on failure". **That reflex is
wrong here, and saying why matters more than the code:**

- for the EU guard, "we could not check" and "there is no conflict" are
  genuinely different populations, and the incident behind it blanked a
  storefront;
- for this guard, **most products have no bound at all**. "Could not read" and
  "none set" are the same population in practice, and refusing every price push
  on a database hiccup would take pricing down for the 99% who never set a floor
  — to protect a bound that probably does not exist.

**The guard's job is to honour a bound that EXISTS, not to gate pricing.** That
is the scepticism-must-be-symmetric rule applied to my own convicting instinct.

### 2.3 Placement and three details

- **Placed beside the quantity guards**, after the push lock and the pause
  checks, so a paused or locked listing still reports *that* rather than a price
  complaint (P4.1c's lesson).
- **A row with no price never touches the database.** A content or quantity push
  must not pay for this guard, nor fail because of it — asserted.
- **A floor above the ceiling is checked FIRST**, on its own terms. With min 100
  and max 85 a price of 90 satisfies neither reading, and reporting "below the
  floor" would send the operator to fix the wrong number.
- **A price of `0` is checked like any other.** A `!price` guard would skip it,
  and zero is exactly the value worth catching.

### 2.4 🔴 A `.catch()` does not cover a synchronous throw

The first version used `prisma.product.findUnique(…).catch(() => null)`. That
covers a rejected promise and **not** a client whose `product` model is absent,
which throws synchronously — so the guard's own promise (*never block a send by
failing*) did not hold for one of the two shapes of failure. It is a `try/catch`
now, and the arm has its own test.

The test that found it could not be written naively either: a
`vi.mock('../db.js', () => ({ default: { product: { findUnique: m.findUnique } } }))`
copies the function **by value** at module-eval time, so taking it away later
changes nothing. The double exposes the model through a **getter**.

## 3. Proof

`price-bounds.p44.vitest.test.ts` — **21 tests**: six "sent" arms (inside,
exactly on the floor, exactly on the ceiling, no bounds, one-sided bounds); the
two refusals with both numbers in the sentence and the right word; the
contradictory-bounds arm; `undefined` / `null` / `NaN` prices left alone; a price
of zero checked; the loader's exact `select`; Decimal-string conversion; **no
database call for a price-free row**; the rejected-promise and the
**synchronous-throw** fail-open arms; and end to end.

Plus a **derived census**: every `priceRefusalFor({` call site must have exactly
one line whose trimmed form starts `if (refusal)` within its block, at least
three sites, and at least three `PRICE_OUT_OF_BOUNDS`. A fourth lane is covered
without editing the test, and matching the **whole condition** is what stops
`if (false)` passing while the word `refusal` survives in the body.

**Mutation checks — 9 of 9 convicted:** the floor unchecked, the ceiling
unchecked, `<` becoming `<=` (caught by the *exactly ON the floor* arm), the
contradictory-bounds check removed, `!price` skipping zero, the no-price
short-circuit removed (caught by the "never touches the database" arm), the
catch rethrowing, and each of two lanes ceasing to act.

**Gates.** `tsc` clean · market-currency ✓ · gateway ratchet all zeros ·
**profiles-ON ratchet ✓** (837 files, none new) · full suite **6 failed /
10,425 passed**, the documented baseline.

## 4. What this does NOT do

- **It does not guard the master price itself.** `masterPriceService.update`
  still accepts any number; the refusal happens at the send. Guarding the save
  would be kinder (the operator learns sooner) and is a separate decision,
  because a bound can be set *after* a price.
- **It does not read `RepricingRule`'s range.** That pair belongs to the
  repricer and already binds it.
- **It cannot be proven by production data.** How many products carry a floor or
  a ceiling in production is unmeasured — Railway database reads are not
  available to this session. The done-when is one refusal carrying
  `PRICE_OUT_OF_BOUNDS`, or a deliberate under-floor save being stopped.
