# SQP.1 — the Brand Analytics feed: why it wrote nothing, and what it actually costs

**2026-08-12 · Phase A (diagnosis) complete. Phase B (§9) built, verified against prod data, and
shipped. §11 is the §6 gate measurement, done — and 🔴 **the session stops there for approval**:
nothing widened, nothing collected, not one report requested. Every Amazon fact here was recovered
from a ledger a real run had already left behind, plus 104 `getReport` metadata reads.**

Cross-links: [KT keyword-tracker page](2026-08-11-kt-keyword-tracker-page.md) §3.4, whose
"I am not guessing at the cause" this replaces.

---

## 1 · The answer

The nightly `sqp-ingest` runs of **2026-08-11** and **2026-08-12** wrote zero rows because
**all 40 of their report requests were abandoned by our own poll loop at its 300-second ceiling.**
Not one report reached `DONE` inside the run.

Then every one of them finished at Amazon, minutes to hours later. Asked directly:

> **104 abandoned reports, all time. `getReport` says DONE = 104. Still generating = 0. FATAL = 0.
> Expired = 0.**

So the reports were never rejected, never fatal, and never empty. They were **generated in full and
thrown away**, because the client had stopped listening before Amazon started working.

## 2 · The run *is* the poll loop

| | 2026-08-11 | 2026-08-12 | 2026-08-10 (control) |
|---|---|---|---|
| reports attempted | 40 | 40 | 40 |
| reached `DONE` in-run | **0** | **0** | 39 |
| abandoned at the ceiling | **40** | **40** | 1 |
| p50 per-report wall time | 315 s | 313 s | 64 s |
| time inside report calls | **210.0 m** | **208.3 m** | 51.0 m |
| total run duration | 210.1 m | 208.4 m | 51.2 m |
| rows written | **0** | **0** | 83 |

The last two rows are the point: **208.3 of the 208.4 minutes** were spent waiting on reports. The
run has no other content. The arithmetic closes exactly — 30 polls × 10 s = 300 s per report, plus
the `createReport` round trip, × 40 reports = 208.7 minutes.

It closes from the HTTP side too. That run made **1,261 `getReport` calls**; 40 reports × the 30-poll
ceiling accounts for 1,200 of them, the remaining 61 belonging to other jobs in the same window.

**So the 3.5-hour runtime and the zero yield are not two problems. They are one measurement of one
defect, reported twice.**

## 3 · Why the reports didn't finish in time: Amazon queues, and does not parallelise

`getReport` returns `processingStartTime` and `processingEndTime`, which separates *waiting* from
*working*. They are different things here, and only one of them is slow.

Sampled from 2026-08-09, consecutive reports:

```
113828…  processing 03:56:23 → 04:11:40
113834…  processing 04:11:40 → 04:11:59
113837…  processing 04:11:59 → 04:27:21
113847…  processing 04:27:21 → 04:27:41
```

Each report begins generating **at the exact second the previous one ends.** Report generation for
this account is **serial — concurrency 1.** Nothing overlaps, ever, in 104 samples.

Which means the delay we measured is queue time, not generation time:

| requested | generation started | queued for | generation took |
|---|---|---|---|
| 08-12 03:45:01 | 08-12 05:08:32 | **83 min** | **7 s** |
| 08-12 07:08:12 | 08-12 09:50:02 | **162 min** | 991 s |
| 08-11 03:45:01 | 08-11 06:14:08 | **149 min** | 2 065 s |
| 08-11 07:09:53 | 08-11 20:49:01 | **13.7 h** | 9 s |

The first row is the whole story in one line: **83 minutes of queueing for 7 seconds of work.**

The 08-11 batch drained from 06:14 to 20:49 — **14.6 hours** for 40 reports. A run that waits 5
minutes per report was never going to catch it, and a run that waited long enough would have to run
for most of a day.

**Consequence: raising the poll ceiling cannot fix this.** The wait is set by queue depth, which our
own burst creates. 40 synchronous report requests against a serial queue is the defect.

Worth noting who else is in that queue: three other report crons run *hourly*
(`FLAT_FILE_RETURNS_DATA_BY_RETURN_DATE`, `FBA_FULFILLMENT_CUSTOMER_RETURNS_DATA`,
`FBA_INVENTORY_PLANNING_DATA`) — roughly 50–70 reports a day competing for the same single slot.

## 4 · The ceiling was always marginal

Across **all 2,244 reports that have ever reached `DONE`**, wall time was:

| p50 | p90 | p99 | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| 33 s | 154 s | **279 s** | 628 s |

The client gives up at 300 s. **It sits between the p99 and the max** — i.e. it was always cutting
off the tail, and 08-11 was the night the whole distribution moved past it. 33 reports have finished
needing more than 250 s; 16 finished needing more than 300 s and survived only because
`createReport` time is counted outside the ceiling.

## 5 · The green row that carries an error message

Phase A's second question — what marks a run *"stale after 2.3h"* while leaving `status=SUCCESS`.

`cron-orphan-sweeper.job.ts` runs every 30 minutes and relabels any `CronRun` still `RUNNING` after
2 hours as `FAILED`, with `errorMessage='stale (auto-swept after Nh)'`. **It does not kill the
process** — it only edits the row. The run continues, and when it finishes,
`cron-observability.ts`'s success branch writes `status=SUCCESS`, `finishedAt` and `outputSummary`
— and **never clears `errorMessage`.** The sweeper's verdict is overwritten; its text is not.

So `SUCCESS` + `stale (auto-swept after 2.3h)` is not a contradiction. It is **a run that blew the
2-hour budget and then completed**, and the error message is a latency measurement.

Exactly two rows in the whole table are in this state, both `sqp-ingest`, true durations 208.4 and
210.1 minutes. Both read green on the dashboard. This is what hid a dead feed for two weeks.

## 6 · Four more things that are measurably wrong

**6.1 · Five of the nine markets it iterates have no Amazon listings at all.** The loop is
`AmazonAdsConnection where isActive=true`, which yields DE, ES, FR, **IE**, IT, **NL**, **PL**,
**SE**, **UK**. The five in bold have **0 matching `ChannelListing` rows**, so `ingestSqp` throws
`no Amazon ASINs` immediately. Every summary for the last 20+ consecutive runs reads
`markets=9 ok=4 failed=5` — a constant, and therefore worthless, `failed` count that a real market
failure would hide inside.

**6.2 · 25 of the 40 reports return genuinely zero rows, every night.** From 2026-08-05 (when
RPT.9 made `rowCount` trustworthy) the split is stable at ~25 empty / ~15 populated. The ASIN
selection is the reason. Of the 10 ASINs `ourAsinsForMarketplace` picks per market, the number that
have *ever* produced an SQP row:

| market | requested | of those, ever produced a row | ASINs in that market that DO produce rows |
|---|---|---|---|
| DE | 10 | **2** | 14 |
| ES | 10 | 7 | 15 |
| FR | 10 | **3** | 5 |
| IT | 10 | **4** | **33** |

IT is the sharp one: 33 of its ASINs are known to return data, and the cron asks about four of them.
The selection comes from `ChannelListing` ordered by `listingStatus`, which has no relationship to
whether Brand Analytics has anything to say.

**6.3 · An abandoned report's `reportId` is discarded.** `failReportRun` records only the error
message, so the column is **null on all 104** abandoned rows. The id survives *only* inside the
error text (`report 115405020677 did not reach DONE…`), which is where this investigation had to
read it from. Nothing can resume a report whose id was never kept — and resuming is the fix.

**6.4 · `NEXUS_ENABLE_SQP_INGEST_CRON` has zero readers.** Three comments name it, including this
job's own header ("Default OFF"). The real gate is the inverted `NEXUS_DISABLE_SQP_INGEST_CRON`, so
the cron is default-**ON** and its header says the opposite.

Two smaller facts, recorded because they bound what any fix can achieve:

- **100 rows is a hard per-report ceiling.** 58 reports returned exactly 100; **none has ever
  returned more.** Widening ASIN coverage buys breadth, never depth — the deepest ASINs stay capped
  at their top 100 queries.
- **`NEXUS_SQP_LOOKBACK=2` keeps the feed structurally ≥11 days stale.** Today it requests
  2026-07-26 → 08-01, a window that closed 11 days ago; `lookback=1` would request 08-02 → 08-08,
  closed 4 days ago. **We have never requested a window newer than 07-26**, so whether Amazon
  serves the 4-day-old week is untested, not known. This is most of the KT page's "share stops
  22–29 days before spend": 7 days of week length + 11 days of lookback + 7 more when the
  completeness gate rejects the thin newest week ≈ 25.

## 7 · Two corrections to my own numbers

**7.1 · "1,549 DONE reports came back empty" was wrong.** My first probe bucketed
`(rowCount ?? 0) === 0`, which is the null-read-as-zero trap. The honest split of the 2,244 DONE
reports is **188 genuinely zero and 1,734 unknown** — `AmazonReportRun.rowCount` is filled by
`deriveRowCount`, which returns `null` for a payload with no top-level array, and RPT.9 only started
populating it on 2026-08-05. Before that date it is null everywhere.

Five runs prove null ≠ empty outright: on 07-30, 07-31, 08-01, 08-02 and 08-03 the ledger sums to
**0 rows** while the run itself upserted **419, 541, 381, 210 and 210**. Where they disagree the run
is right: `parseSqp` reads the payload, `deriveRowCount` only guesses at its shape. **Read `status`
and `errorMessage` from that ledger; never `rowCount` before 08-05.**

**7.2 · "Amazon's generation latency drifted up into our ceiling" was wrong.** It is a step change,
not a drift. On 08-10, 39 of 40 reports finished, max 203 s, **none above 250 s**. The next night,
0 of 40 finished within 300 s. And generation time was never the variable — queue time was.

One more, about the instrument the KT page pointed at: `GET /advertising/sqp/debug` **could not have
answered this**. `sqpDebugState` is written only after a report *returns*, inside the loop body that
a failed fetch `continue`s past — so on both zero-yield nights it was never written at all, and the
endpoint would have served a stale sample from days earlier.

## 9 · Phase B — built, and verified against prod data

Four changes, none of which alters what is requested from Amazon. All four are in; the verification
below is `_sqp1-verify.mts` run against the live database, not a reading of the diff.

**9.1 · The market list now comes from where ASINs exist**, not from `AmazonAdsConnection.isActive`.
Measured: 9 candidates → **4 eligible (DE, ES, FR, IT), 5 skipped (IE, NL, PL, SE, UK)** — exactly
the four markets that have ever produced an SQP row. The ASIN list is resolved once and passed into
`ingestSqp`, so the market filter and the reports it requests cannot disagree. Skipped markets are
**named** in the summary; dropping them silently would have recreated the same blindness the
constant `failed=5` had.

**9.2 · The summary is honest, and a zero-row run is no longer green.** On the numbers the last two
nights actually produced:

```
OLD  markets=9 ok=4 failed=5 rows=0                              ← and status=SUCCESS
NEW  markets=4 skipped=5[IE,NL,PL,SE,UK] · reports=40 failed=40 abandoned=40 · parsed=0 rows=0
     · DE 0/10 done (10 abandoned) 0 rows · ES 0/10 … · FR 0/10 … · IT 0/10 …
     → FAILED: "every one of the 40 reports was ABANDONED at the ~300s poll ceiling; they do
        finish at Amazon afterwards, so this is a collection failure and not an Amazon failure"
```

And on 2026-08-10, the last night that worked, the same code stays green:
`reports=40 failed=1 abandoned=1 · parsed=83 rows=83 · … IT 9/10 done (1 abandoned) 6 rows` → SUCCESS.

Two couplings had to be honoured rather than discovered later:

- **`rows=` keeps its name.** `keyword-tracker.service.ts` parses `/rows=(\d+)/` out of this string
  to drive the KT page's feed-health line. Renaming it to `upserted=` type-checks, builds and passes
  every other test while silently zeroing a defect signal. `parsed=` is the new, separate number.
- **The reader now tries `errorMessage` too.** `recordCronRun` persists `outputSummary` only on the
  success path, so making a zero-row run throw moves its summary into `errorMessage` — and a reader
  that only looked at `outputSummary` would have scored exactly the runs this signal exists to catch
  as "no claim". That is the fix reporting itself as the absence of the defect.

Both are pinned by `sqp-ingest-summary.vitest.test.ts` (11 tests, no mocks — the numbers are the
measured 08-10 and 08-12 outcomes). The cross-file guard was **seen to fail first**: renaming the
token breaks 4 tests, and the KT reader's regex returns `null` in exactly the way that would have
gone unnoticed in production.

**9.3 · `failReportRun` records the `reportId`.** A new `SpApiReportError` carries the id out of the
two throw sites that know it, and marks whether the report was abandoned at our ceiling or ended by
Amazon. 🔴 **This is the one change that cannot be verified before the next real run** — it needs a
report to be abandoned. The before-picture is recorded: 104 abandoned rows, **0** with an id in the
column. Re-run `_sqp1-verify.mts` after the next nightly to confirm it fills.

**9.4 · The decoy is retired** from `sqp-ingest.job.ts`'s header and `index.ts`, both of which
claimed `NEXUS_ENABLE_SQP_INGEST_CRON` meant "default OFF". 🔴 **The variable is still set to `1` in
Railway with no reader anywhere** — which is worse than absent, because it reads as proof the feed is
deliberately on. Deleting it is an env change and therefore the operator's call, so it is flagged
here rather than done.

What Phase B deliberately does **not** fix: the abandonment itself. That is §10.

## 10 · The real fix, which is a design change and not Phase B

**Collect asynchronously.** Request the reports, record the ids, return. A later pass polls
outstanding ids and ingests whatever is `DONE`.

This is not a preference; it is what the measurement forces. The queue is serial and its depth is
set by our own burst, so no in-run wait is long enough — the 08-11 batch needed 14.6 hours. Async
collection also makes the run short, retires the sweeper collision, and turns every abandoned report
back into data instead of waste. It needs a decision before it is built.

**11.3 · 🔴 The number the engine steers on is derived from one ASIN, in most campaigns.**

This was not on the brief's list and it changes what the gate is about.
`sqpImpressionShareForAsins` takes `rows[0].startDate` as "latest" and sums **only that week**. The
week is therefore chosen by whichever single ASIN has the freshest row, and every ASIN whose newest
data is older is dropped from the average without trace. Measured across the 34 campaigns with a
signal:

- **On average 10% of a campaign's ASINs contribute** to the share it is steered by.
- **31 of 34 are steered by a quarter or fewer** of their ASINs.
- **22 of 34 are steered by exactly ONE ASIN** — including all five DE campaigns above, whose
  0.965% is one ASIN's share.
- 13 of the 16 groups draw on ASINs whose newest weeks span up to **six different weeks**
  (2026-06-21 … 2026-07-26), so which week gets used is an accident of coverage.

Two consequences for the gate, pulling in opposite directions and both real:

1. Widening **helps this** — more ASINs present in the newest week means more of them contribute.
2. But the collapse is **structural**, not a coverage artefact: coverage is never even (25 of 40
   reports return genuinely zero rows), so the max-week filter will always silently drop laggards.
   And while it holds, **a single new report can swing a campaign's share by 4×** — which makes the
   projections in 11.2 unstable in a way no amount of measurement here can settle.

🔴 `sqpImpressionShareForAsins` is explicitly out of scope for this session, so it is **measured and
reported, not touched.** But the gate decision should know that widening the feed and fixing the
reader are entangled: widened data flowing into the current reader would move live rank bids by
multiples, off a basis of one ASIN.

**11.4 · The cost, priced in reports rather than minutes.**

81 distinct ENABLED ASINs across rank-governed campaigns — but SQP is requested **per (market,
ASIN)**, so full coverage is **81 IT + 18 DE = 99 report requests per week**, not 81. (The probe
prints 81; that is the distinct-ASIN count and undercounts the request volume, since the EU
catalogue is shared. Corrected here.) Coverage today: IT **19 of 81 ASINs ever measured (23%)**,
DE 13 of 18 (72%).

Against the serial queue measured in §3:

| reports | at p50 generation (33 s) | at p90 (154 s) |
|---|---|---|
| 40 (today) | 0.4 h | 1.7 h |
| 99 (full coverage) | 0.9 h | 4.2 h |

And ~50–70 non-SQP reports a day already share that single slot. **So full coverage is not
schedulable as one nightly synchronous pass at any poll ceiling** — it needs §10's asynchronous
collector first, and probably rotation on top. That ordering is a finding of this gate, not a
preference.

**11.5 · The 100-row ceiling bounds what coverage can buy.** 58 reports have returned exactly 100
rows and **none has ever exceeded it**, so widening buys breadth and never depth.

**11.6 · Still unmeasured, and only answerable by asking Amazon.** Whether `lookback=1` (the week
that closed 4 days ago) is served at all. It has never been requested, so the feed's ≥11-day
staleness has never been tested — one report would settle it, and that request belongs on the far

# SQP.2 — the 80 reports, staged and measured

**2026-08-12, same day. Everything above stands. What changed is that the reports are now on disk
and the question of what they contain is answered rather than assumed — and the answer overturns two
premises, including one of mine.**

## 13 · Phase A — staged, before anything else

`_sqp2-stage.mts`, `getReport` + `getReportDocument` + download only. No report created, nothing
written to the database.

| | |
|---|---|
| recoverable report ids | **104 of 104** (from the error text; the column was null before SQP.1) |
| documents downloaded | **104** |
| expired / still generating / terminal / errors | **0 / 0 / 0 / 0** |
| rows parsed | **148** across 28 reports; 76 reports parsed to zero |

The raw document is kept beside the parsed NDJSON, with a sha256 and byte count per report. That was

## 14 · 🔴 Three things that turned out not to be true

**14.1 · A zero-row parse does not implicate the parser.** The stop condition said it would, on the
grounds that SQP.1 proved these reports are not empty. SQP.1 proved they reached `DONE`; it
separately measured (§6.2) that **25 of 40 come back genuinely empty** because the ASIN selection asks
about ASINs Brand Analytics holds nothing on. The first document staged settled it in twenty seconds:

```json
{ "reportSpecification": { "reportOptions": { "asin": "B0BVQLQY1D", ... } },
  "dataByAsin": [ ] }
```

334 bytes, a literal empty array — and `B0BVQLQY1D` is one of the six IT ASINs SQP.1 measured as
never having produced a row. 76 of 104 are like this; the other 28 carry 148 rows, so the parser
demonstrably works. **Halting there would have burned the irreplaceable download window on a false
alarm.** The condition that would actually implicate the parser is *all* of them parsing to zero,
which cannot be a property of an account; `_sqp2-stage.mts` now reports it that way.

**14.2 · The 07-26 week does not go from 85 rows to ~1,200. It gains ZERO rows.**

Measured by keying the staged rows exactly as the upsert does
(`marketplace, reportPeriod, startDate, searchQuery, asin`):

| market | week | new rows | changed values | already identical | stored now → after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DE | 2026-07-26 | 0 | 0 | 10 | 5 → 5 |
| ES | 2026-07-26 | 0 | 0 | 106 | 71 → 71 |
| FR | 2026-07-26 | 0 | 0 | 3 | 1 → 1 |
| IT | 2026-07-26 | 0 | 0 | 12 | 8 → 8 |
| **FR** | **2026-07-19** | **15** | 0 | 0 | **4 → 19** |
| **ES** | **2026-06-14** | 0 | **2** | 0 | 465 → 465 |

**All 131 staged rows for 07-26 are byte-identical to what is already stored**, because the 08-09 and
08-10 runs already collected the same ten ASINs for the same week. The entire recovery is worth
**15 new FR rows in the 07-19 week and 2 corrected ES values in 06-14.**

So the week is thin **at Amazon**, not thin because we failed to collect it. A healthy week reaches
~1,200 rows by having many ASINs return ~100 queries each; for 07-26 these ASINs return 0–26. The
deadline was real but what it was protecting was not.

*(A defect in my own gate probe, caught before it was reported: the per-market rollup added FR's 15
creates — which belong to 07-19 — to FR's 07-26 count and printed "1 → 16". It is now keyed by
(market, period). A per-market rollup of a per-period fact invents exactly the number the gate turns
on.)*

**14.3 · Retention does not run from the request.** Documents requested **170.5 hours** earlier
downloaded fine; so did the 89-hour and 64-hour batches. Nothing 404'd at any age.

| request batch | downloaded | age at download |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-05 | 7 | **170.5 h** |
| 2026-08-09 | 16 | 89.0 h |
| 2026-08-11 | 40 | 41.2 h |
| 2026-08-12 | 40 | 17.2 h |

The ~72 h clock therefore starts when Amazon **creates the document**, which for a queued report is
hours after we asked. Two consequences: §8's "expires ~2026-08-14" was conservative, and an age-based
`EXPIRED` verdict in the collector would retire requests whose documents are still sitting there —
losing precisely what the design exists to protect. **The collector now concludes expiry only from a
404.** Age is reported as a warning.

## 16 · Phase D — shipped: request and collect are now separate passes

- **`sqp-ingest`** creates the reports, records them, and returns in seconds. `rows=0` is correct for
  this pass and does not throw; the honest failure is "created nothing", which is what it checks.
  `NEXUS_SQP_SYNCHRONOUS_INGEST=1` restores the old behaviour for comparison only.
- **`sqp-collect`** (new, hourly at :20, offset from the :00 report crons) polls outstanding ids and
  ingests whatever is `DONE`. `INGESTED` / `DONE` / `FATAL` / `CANCELLED` / `EXPIRED` / `PENDING`
  stay six distinct outcomes — `stillPending` is normal, not a failure, and only a real expiry fails
  the row.
- **`SqpReportRequest`** — one additive table, nothing altered. It is deliberately not
  `AmazonReportRun`: that registry records what a puller *did*, in Amazon's status vocabulary, and
  feeds the freshness surface. This records what we are still *waiting for*, in ours.
- `getSpApiClient` is exported from `sp-api-reports.service.ts` so a caller can drive the three
  report operations separately. `fetchSpApiReport` (create→poll→download in one call) is exactly the
  shape that cannot work here.

### 16.1 · 🔴 The capacity bound — 65 reports/day, and we are at 61

**Measured, not modelled.** The first version of `sqpCapacity` modelled it from generation times and
returned a sustainable capacity of **−17**, because it applied SQP's bimodal p90 (1,450 s; 55 of 90
samples are under 60 s and 35 are over ten minutes) to report types whose generation time was never
measured at all. Flat-file returns reports do not take 24 minutes. It was replaced by the one thing
observed end to end: **40 reports drained in 14.6 h** under the normal competing load.

| | |
|---|---|
| observed throughput | **2.74 reports/hour** |
| **sustainable SQP reports/day** | **65** — a batch must clear inside the 24 h before the next |
| pulled today | **60.9/day** (852 in 14 days) |
| **headroom** | **4.1 reports/day** |

| scenario | reports | drain | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| today's nightly ask | 40 | 14.6 h | clears within the day |
| full coverage of rank-governed campaigns | 99 | 36.1 h | **cannot clear — compounds nightly** |
| every advertised IT ASIN | 250 | 91.3 h | **cannot clear** |

**The account is already at its ceiling.** Widening has no room without reducing the competing load
(~65 non-SQP reports/day: returns and FBA planning, hourly) or accepting a backlog that grows every
night. That is the runaway which produced the two zero-yield nights in the first place.

### 16.2 · Pacing does not help, and history already ran the experiment

| day | reports | mean gap between requests | drain span |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-11 | 40 | 5.3 min | 3.4 h of requests → 14.6 h of drain |
| 2026-08-12 | 40 | 5.2 min | same |

The abandoned poll loop spaced each `createReport` ~5.2 minutes apart — an unintentional but real
paced run. The batch still took 14.6 h. Serial generation does not care when the requests arrived,
and since retention starts at document creation (14.3), pacing buys nothing for expiry either. So the
collector paces its *reads* only, to stay clear of the throttle path.

2. **The reader fix (§11.3 / Phase E)** — unchanged and now clearly the critical path: while

3. **Widening** — blocked by 16.1 before it is blocked by anything else. There is room for 4 reports

4. **Phase D at nightly scale.** The mechanism is verified end to end on prod, with one real report

## 18 · Phase E — three reader options, measured, handed to RD

`_sqp2-reader.mts`. Measurement only; nothing shipped, and `sqpImpressionShareForAsins` is untouched.
The local reader was spot-checked against the real function on 6 campaigns (0 mismatches), on top of
`_sqp2-gate.mts` §C's 45-campaign validation.

**Baseline:** 34 of 45 campaigns have a share; 20 are steered by exactly one ASIN, 31 by ≤3; the mean
campaign contributes **10%** of its ASINs to its own number; every signal comes from a week **17–24
days old.**

**Option A · minimum contributors** — null unless ≥N ASINs are in the chosen week:

| N | keep a signal | go null | mean contributing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 34 of 34 | 0 | 10% (today) |
| **2** | **14 of 34** | **20** | 15% |
| 3 | 3 of 34 | 31 | 53% |
| 5 | 3 of 34 | 31 | 53% |

N=2 already drops 20 campaigns, because the newest week is the thin one. So this mostly converts a bad
number into *no* number — correct as a safety property, but it is a **coverage fix wearing a reader
fix's clothes**: the basis is thin because the feed is thin.

**Option B · window** — pool the last N weeks rather than the single latest:

| weeks | with a signal | mean contributing | median \|Δ\| vs today |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 34 of 45 | 10% | 0.000pp |
| 2 | 34 of 45 | **44%** | 0.132pp |
| 4 | 34 of 45 | 53% | 0.126pp |
| 6 | 34 of 45 | 61% | 0.124pp |

Two weeks quadruples the contributing fraction — the biggest improvement available — and moves the
value by ~0.13pp, which is large next to shares of 0.15–0.97%. It is not free: it reintroduces the
mixed-week average KT.1b spent a session removing from the page ("one period per view"). If RD takes
it, the number needs a label saying it spans N weeks, or the page and the engine will mean different
things by "share".

**Option C · recency guard** — null when the chosen week is older than N days:

| N days | keep a signal | go null |
|---|---|---|
| 14 | **0 of 34** | 34 |
| 21 | 31 of 34 | 3 |
| 28 / 42 | 34 of 34 | 0 |

With `NEXUS_SQP_LOOKBACK=2` the feed can never be fresher than ~11 days plus the week length, so a
guard tighter than ~21 days nulls everything permanently. At 28 days it changes nothing today — which
is exactly what a stall alarm should do.

### 18.1 · 🔴 What the engine does with a null — and why none of the above matters yet

`computeStep` runs: pause → below-floor → **`!canChase`** → all-out → loss → **IS branch (needs
`targetISPct` AND a non-null share)** → ACOS branch → hold.

So **a null is not neutral**: it skips the IS branch and falls through to the ACOS branch, which
*raises* when ACoS is under 80% of cap. Any option that nulls more campaigns is therefore not
automatically safe, and Option A needs that fall-through reviewed alongside it.

Except it is all unreachable. Measured per campaign on the real specs:

> **campaigns where the IS branch is reachable: 0. Where it is not: 45.**

**So the reader is not the critical path — the BAND is.** `maxBiasPct` is null on all five
`RankTarget`s, and no campaign override survives today, so `!canChase` returns before any share is
read. Until a `maxBiasPct` is set, no reader change and no feed change can move a bid.

### 18.2 · Recommendation to RD, in order

1. **Decide the band first.** Everything else is inert until `maxBiasPct` is set on a target.
2. **Then Option A at N=2**, as a safety property — a one-ASIN basis should not steer a bid, and
   refusing to answer is the honest failure. Review the ACOS fall-through at the same time.
3. **Option B at 2 weeks** if coverage stays thin, but only with an explicit "spans N weeks" label.
4. **Option C at 28 days** as a stall alarm. It changes nothing today; that is the point.

# SQP.3 — three unknowns settled, and two earlier conclusions overturned

**2026-08-13. Phase A only: measurement. Nothing was built, no schedule changed, no limit raised,
and nothing was written to `SearchQueryPerformance`.** Five + six reports were requested from Amazon
purely to compare against what is stored. Probes: `_sqp3-state`, `_sqp3-cadence`, `_sqp3-pick`,
`_sqp3-experiments`, `_sqp3-queue2`, `_sqp3-zerobrand`, `_sqp3-throughput`.

## S1 · Two things in the brief that were already false

**S1.1 · `sqp-collect` does not run 1.1×/day. It runs 22×/day.** It is scheduled `20 * * * *` and the
cron history shows 13:20, 14:20, 15:20, 16:20, 17:20, 18:20… The "1.1/day" figure is what you get by
dividing the run count by days *since the job was created* rather than counting a window.

**And its latency needs no tuning:** measured across all 41 `SqpReportRequest` rows,
request → ingest is **p50 0.4 h, p90 0.9 h, max 1.0 h** — bounded by the hourly tick exactly as
designed. Phase B item 1 has nothing to fix.

**S1.2 · `createReport` is the request pass's real bound, not the generation queue.** Last night's
pass took **2,629 s for 40 `createReport` calls = 65.7 s each**, which is Amazon's documented ~1/min
rate limit. Firing six concurrently confirmed it: three landed within 1.2 s (the burst) and the rest
were pushed to +61 s, +185 s and +364 s. **That throttle is shared across report types** — the Sales
& Traffic creates queued behind the Brand Analytics ones.

## S2 · 🔴 4.1 — Re-fetching does NOT revise. And the "57%" was our own bug.

Same ASIN (**IT `B0BMSWM15B`**, 100 rows in each of 7 weeks — chosen because ranking by span alone
first picked an FR ASIN with *one* row in its newest week, which cannot detect anything). Three ages,
compared field by field against what is stored:

| week | age | fresh rows | stored rows | **differing** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-07-19 | 25 d | 100 | 100 | **0** |
| 2026-06-28 | 46 d | 100 | 100 | **0** |
| 2026-06-07 | 67 d | 100 | 100 | 100 — *see below* |

**The decisive control:** on the 46-day week, `updatedAt > ingestedAt` on **100 of 100** stored rows
while **0 of 100** values differed. That is the direct disproof of the "57% revised" claim — the
timestamp moved on every row and not one number did. Prisma's `@updatedAt` fires on the update path
whether or not a value changes, and `ingestSqp`'s update branch writes every field every time, so it
measures **re-fetch**, never **revision**.

**And the 67-day week's 100 differences are not revision either.** Every one was in the same
direction: `impressionsBrand 0 → 50`, `clicksBrand 0 → 3`. The **stored** value is zero and the
**fresh** value is real — that is `parseSqp`'s own documented ACR.0.2 defect being repaired by the
current parser, not Amazon restating anything.

**⇒ A week is FROZEN. Re-fetching a settled week returns byte-identical data and buys nothing.**
Last night's run re-requested 2026-07-26 for the fifth time.

### S2.1 · The parser debt this exposed — 3,700 rows

| | |
|---|---|
| `impressionsTotal > 0` **and** `impressionsBrand = 0` | **3,700** 🔴 |
| `impressionsTotal > 0` and `impressionsBrand > 0` | 11,375 |
| `impressionsTotal = 0` | 0 |

By week, the cutover is clean: everything ingested **before ~2026-06-28 is 100% broken**

`impressionShare` is stored as `impressionsBrand / impressionsTotal`, so every one of those rows has a
share of exactly **0** — a stored zero that was never real. SOV.0 recorded that *"a rounded 0.00% is
not a zero"*; this is its other half.

**How much of it is live:** `sqpImpressionShareForAsins` reads the newest week per ASIN with no date
floor, and **2 of 67 (market, ASIN) pairs** have a 100%-broken week as their newest —
`FR B0CFYNY31T` (05-24) and `IT B0BTCDLLB5` (05-31). Those two read a share of exactly 0 today. The
other 3,698 rows sit outside the KT page's 42-day window and are invisible. **Real debt, nearly
dormant, and repairable by re-fetch — which is now known to be the one thing re-fetching is good for.**

## S3 · 🔴 4.2 — The queue is PER REPORT TYPE. SQP.2 got this wrong.

The first attempt could not settle it and is reported as such: `createReport`'s 65 s throttle meant the

BA-B0BMSWM15B   18:43:48 → 18:44:30   (42 s)

**Two clean cross-type overlaps.** Sales & Traffic generates while Brand Analytics is generating.
Brand Analytics remains serial *within itself* (each start within a second of the previous end —

### S3.1 · 🔴 And "2.74 reports/hour" is not a constant

|---|---|---|---|

## S4 · 🔴 4.3 — `SQP_LOOKBACK = 1` works

## S5 · What Phase B should therefore do

1. **`SQP_LOOKBACK` 2 → 1.** The cheapest change available: seven days fresher, no extra reports.

2. **Stop re-fetching settled weeks.** A week is frozen (S2), so requesting 2026-07-26 a sixth time
   is 40 reports of pure waste. Fetch a week until it stops changing, then leave it.
3. **`sqp-collect`'s cadence: no change.** It already runs hourly with p50 0.4 h latency (S1.1).
4. **The capacity story changes but does not become a blank cheque** (S3.1). Widening is not blocked
   by a 4.1/day ceiling that does not exist, but it must be sized for the slow night.

**Nothing above is built yet.** Phase A was measurement, and this is the report the brief asks for
before anything changes.

## SQP.3 · Phase B — what shipped, and the defect the verification found

**Three changes, each following from a Phase A measurement.** Verified on production by running the
real request → collect cycle, not by reading the code.

### B1 · `SQP_LOOKBACK` 2 → 1 — seven days of freshness, live

Verified on prod: the pass now targets **2026-08-02** (0 rows stored) instead of 2026-07-26 (85). The
cycle then filled it — **66 rows across 2 ASINs**, and `newest week stored anywhere` moved from
2026-07-26 to **2026-08-02**.

🔴 **And the job carried its own copy of the default.** `sqp-ingest.job.ts` computed its window with a
second `Math.max(1, Number(process.env.NEXUS_SQP_LOOKBACK) || 2)`, so the lookback had two independent
definitions and changing the one in `sqp.service.ts` would have had **no effect on the job that
actually requests the reports**. Now imported from the one definition.

### B2 · Stop re-requesting a week that has stopped moving

Settledness needs change detection, and neither of the two things that look like change detection
works: `rowsUpserted` counts writes, not differences, and `updatedAt` moved on 100 of 100 rows while 0
of 100 values did (§4.1). So `collectSqpReports` now reads the prior rows and **compares values**,
recording `SqpReportRequest.rowsChanged` (additive column, applied to prod).

A **NULL** `rowsChanged` means *we don't know* and is never read as *nothing changed* — the 41
requests that predate the column re-fetch rather than settle on an assumption.

### B3 · 🔴 The defect the first prod cycle exposed, and the fix

The cycle proved the mechanism end to end:

| pass | created | ingested | **rowsChanged** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | 66 rows | **66** — all new |
| 2 | 2 | 66 rows | **0** — the week is frozen |
| 3 | **0** | — | *2 already settled; Amazon not called* |

**And that is the bug.** Pass 2 confirmed "nothing changed" **three minutes** after pass 1, and that
was enough to freeze a five-day-old week permanently. §4.1 measured weeks frozen at 25 and 46 days; it
never measured whether a five-day-old week is still filling in on day ten. A burst of two fetches is
not evidence that a week has stopped filling — it is evidence that we asked twice quickly.

`SQP_SETTLE_MIN_SPAN_HOURS = 20` now requires the agreeing fetch to span a day boundary. In production
this costs nothing — the pass runs nightly, so consecutive fetches are ~24h apart already — but it
makes the rule mean *"this week did not move overnight"*.

Checked against the rows the cycle actually wrote:

```
B0BMSWM15B: confirmations span 0.01h
B0BMS6ZZ4H: confirmations span 0.01h
settled WITH the 20h guard:  0 (none)
settled with NO guard:       2   ← what would have frozen
```

Six tests pin it, including the boundary either side of the constant and the NULL branch.

### B4 · `sqp-collect` cadence — deliberately unchanged

It already runs hourly at p50 0.4h (§S1.1). Two earlier prompts recommended changing it on an
unproven hypothesis; the measurement says there is nothing to change.

### Still unmeasured, and stated as such

Whether a week revises between **day 5 and day 25** is not measured. Phase A covers 25d and 46d, and
the cycle covers minutes at day 5. The 20h guard is the conservative response to that gap, not a
substitute for measuring it — settling now requires a week to hold still overnight, and the guard can
be raised without touching anything else if a revision is ever observed.

### B6 · 🔴 The mistake that crashed the API deploy, and what it cost

Applying the column to prod by hand with `ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS` and then shipping a migration with
a **bare** `ADD COLUMN` crashed the deploy with `42701`, which Prisma escalates to **P3009 — new
migrations will not be applied**. That is service-wide: the next deploy after mine would have been
blocked too, whoever pushed it and whatever it contained.

Recovered by marking the migration applied (the column exists with exactly the intended shape, so this
is truthful rather than a paper-over), confirmed with `prisma migrate status` → *"Database schema is up
to date"*, and the file now carries `IF NOT EXISTS`.

**The general rule this earns:** a hand-applied schema change and its migration file must be written to
agree, because Prisma does not treat "already done" as success — and the blast radius of getting it
wrong is every other service deploy, not just your own.
