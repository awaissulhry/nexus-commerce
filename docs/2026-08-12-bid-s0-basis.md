## 2 · 🔴 Three findings S1–S9 must build on

### 2.1 The metric columns are blank on 82% of rows, and that is real

| | |
|---|---|
| ENABLED positive targets | **2,944** |
| …carrying **any** 30-day `AmazonAdsDailyPerformance` row | **521 — 17.7%** |
| …with impressions > 0 | 509 |
| …with a click, or a cent of spend | **274** |

Those 2,423 targets were never served. The data is not missing; there is nothing to report. But a
grid rendering `—` five times on 2,423 rows teaches an operator that the page is broken, and an
operator who has learned that stops trusting the columns that *are* populated.

So the page distinguishes **three** states where a naive grid has one:

| state | renders | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| no performance row in the window | *not served* (italic, muted) | never entered an auction |
| measured, denominator zero | `—` | served, but no clicks (CPC) or no sales (ACoS) |
| measured, value present | the number | — |

`measured` is a row field, a facet with a count, and a census cell. One sentence above the grid
states the ratio.

**What this binds:** S2's sparkline will be empty on five rows in six, and S3's bid curve will open
on a target with no metric history far more often than not. Neither should treat that as an error
state. The number that makes any of this move is `NEXUS_BID_OPTIMIZER_SOURCE` — still unset, still
not a UI decision.

### 2.2 A bid is only live if the campaign is live — an intersection, not a status

| | |
|---|---|
| campaigns holding an ENABLED positive target | **217** |
| …that are themselves ENABLED | **83** |
| ENABLED targets **in** an ENABLED campaign | **1,091** |
| ENABLED targets in a paused or archived campaign | **1,853 — 63%** |

1,853 bids enter no auction and no bidder will ever move them. This is the same mistake NEG.1 found
on "blocking now": counting a row that is switched on inside something switched off. Every target
row carries `liveNow`, the campaign cell carries a status chip, and the census carries *bidding now*.

**What this binds:** every population figure any later section renders — "N at the floor", "N above
their ceiling", "N with no bidder" — has to say whether it means enabled targets or *live* ones. The
two differ by a factor of 2.7. The page study's §4 numbers are campaign-level and unaffected; its
§6 "57 live bids sit above their campaign's ceiling" should be re-derived against `liveNow`.

### 2.3 The floor population is a clock reading, and now we have both ends of the swing

| when | targets at ≤5¢ |
|---|---|
| 2026-08-11 14:00 Rome (study) | 141 keywords |
| 2026-08-12 00:10 Rome (this session) | **647 targets** |

The study warned the population "swings ~400 between 00:00 and 08:00". Confirmed, and the swing is
larger than that at the all-kinds grain. Any count of the bottom band is true for the minute it was
read, so the page stamps the time on it.

**What this binds:** S2 and S5 own the *labelling* — the three different kinds of 2¢ keyword
(suppressed with a restore value · in a min-bid window · at the floor with no memory). S0
deliberately does not claim which is which. A band chip is a filter, not a diagnosis.

### And two smaller ones

- **Match types: 13 live values, not 3.** EXACT 789 · PRODUCT_EXACT 661 · PHRASE 604 · BROAD 589 ·
  PRODUCT_SIMILAR 136 · UNKNOWN 52 · SEARCH_CLOSE_MATCH 27 · SEARCH_LOOSE_MATCH 26 ·
  PRODUCT_SUBSTITUTES 25 · PRODUCT_COMPLEMENTS 19 · AUTO 8 · SEARCH_RELATED_TO_YOUR_LANDING_PAGES 4 ·
  SEARCH_RELATED_TO_YOUR_BRAND 4. The brief's `EXACT|PHRASE|BROAD` would have hidden 1,551 rows
  behind a filter that looked complete. **Kinds: 7, not 4.** Both filters are server facets with
  counts, and the route validates neither against an enum — an unknown value returns nothing rather
  than being rejected, because Amazon adds targeting forms.
- **One campaign spent €453.93 in 30 days with every one of its 11 targets pinned at €0.02**
  (`GALE EXACT DE`). Not S0's to explain — but it is the single most interesting row the grid
  surfaced on its first run, and it is what S1's bidder band and S8's activity list exist for.

## 3 · The URL contract

Every param round-trips, back/forward walks the history, and a copied link reproduces the view.

```
/marketing/ads/rules-automation/bid
  ?market=IT|DE|FR|ES|all        default all — the HEADER's picker, not the scope bar's
  &line=<productId>              a Product parent id (the product line)
  &portfolio=<externalId>        Amazon's external portfolio id, not a local row id
  &campaign=<campaignId>
  &view=targets|campaigns        default targets
  &kind=<value>                  any live AdTarget.kind — 7 today, facet-driven
  &match=<value>                 any live expressionType — 13 today, facet-driven
  &band=0-5|6-20|21-50|51-100|100+
  &measured=yes|no|all           default all — added in S0, see §2.1
  &status=enabled|paused|archived|all    default enabled — added in S0, see below
  &q=<search>
  &sort=<col>:<asc|desc>
  &window=7d|30d|60d             default 30d. The METRIC window, not the history window.
```

A param at its default is **deleted** from the URL, so the address bar stays short and two identical
views always have identical URLs.

**`&status=` was added in S0** (approved). Without it the page either silently hides 210 rows or
silently shows archived ones. `enabled` + `paused` + `archived` = 3,154, verified.

**Sort keys.** targets: `target · match · kind · adGroup · campaign · market · bid · impressions ·
clicks · cpc · spend · acos`. campaigns: `campaign · market · targets · bidRange · spend · sales ·
acos`. A null metric sorts **last in both directions** — see §5.

### 🔴 Reserved. Parsed into the slot contract, read by nobody, do not repurpose.

| param | owner | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `bidder=` | **S6** | `schedule \| goal \| manual \| none` |
| `state=` | **S2** | `at-floor \| suppressed \| out-of-band \| unexplained \| failing` |
| `target=` | **S3** | one `AdTarget.id` — opens the bid-curve drawer |

They are in `BidSlotProps.reserved` today so a link shared before the section lands still carries
its meaning when it arrives.

## 4 · The refresh hook

```ts
useCursorPoll({ url, params, baseline, intervalMs = 45_000, enabled = true })
  → { cursor, stale, lastCheckedAt, check }
```

`GET /advertising/bid-grid/cursor?<scope params>` → `{ targetsAt, loggedAt, n }`, ~100 bytes,
`Cache-Control: no-store` (a cached cursor cannot detect a change, which is its only job).

### 🔴 Why `targetsAt` and not the audit log

Measured 2026-08-12 00:30 Rome:

```
newest AdvertisingActionLog AD_BID_UPDATE   2026-08-11T22:16:50Z
newest AdTarget.updatedAt                   2026-08-12T00:30:58Z     ← 134 minutes ahead
```

That gap is writer 9 from the page study — `ads-keyword-bid-resync`, the hourly inbound sync that
overwrites `bidCents` with Amazon's value and writes **no** `AdvertisingActionLog` row and **no**
`CampaignBidHistory` row. A cursor watching only the audit spine would sit reporting "nothing
changed" through every bid edit made in Seller Central. `n` is the third field because neither
timestamp moves on a create or a delete.

**Not SSE.** That bus carries 0.21% of writes and the engines publish nothing to it.

### Three rules it follows

1. **Never yank rows out from under a reader.** A change sets `stale` and offers a button.
2. **Do not poll a hidden tab.** Pauses on `visibilitychange`, checks immediately on return and on
   window focus.
3. **A failed poll is silent.** The cursor going down must not put an error on a page whose data
   loaded fine.

### The lift

Nothing in `useCursorPoll.ts` names a bid: it takes a URL, a params object and a baseline, and
returns four fields. Moving the file to `_shared/` is the entire shared-layer change. The
`loggedAt` half is account-wide rather than scope-filtered (the action log stores `AD_TARGET` ids
with no campaign column) — it can only over-report, costing one refetch, and the alternative
over-reports the other way.

## 5 · What building it changed about the plan

**`showMarket` does not exist.** The brief asked for `AdsPageHeader … showMarket={false}`. The prop
has zero hits repo-wide; it was reverted with the scope bar on 2026-08-10 and session-locks §2
already carried the correction. The page owns one market state, passes it to the header, and the
scope bar renders three grains and never a fourth.

**`automations/ScopeForm.tsx` cannot be reused, and I stopped and said so.** It is not a filter bar
— it is the rule-scope *binding editor*, it holds `scope*Id` fields and ends in a "Bind this scope"
button that writes to a rule. What exists instead are two near-identical page-local forks
(`KeywordScopeBar`, `NegativeScopeBar`); `BidScopeBar` is the third, because extracting a shared one
means editing two other sessions' directories. **Recorded in session-locks §4.** The genuinely
shared thing is on the server: `resolveScopeReach()` already resolves all four grains and is what
the rule evaluator enforces with — this page calls it, so "the campaigns this view shows" and "the
campaigns a rule scoped this way reaches" are the same set by construction. The twelfth pass should
extract one bar and point it at that resolver.

**`GET /advertising/targets` could not serve the grid.** `take: 2000` against 3,154 positive rows
with **no `orderBy`**; a single `campaignId` and no market/portfolio/line/status filter; no
aggregate. One new read instead, in `advertising-intel.routes.ts` — the 600 KB file was not touched.
RBAC needed no edit: `permissions-manifest.ts:214` maps any GET under `/api/advertising` to
`adsView` and precedes the `/bid` → `adsBidsEdit` rule, first-match-wins. Confirmed by the push's own
coverage gate (0 unmapped) and by 401 on both routes.

**`AdsDataGrid` was linkable inward and unlinkable outward.** `defaultSort` and `initialFilters`
could seed it from a URL; nothing could get a change back, because `onSort` is internal. So on every
page using this grid, clicking a header or a filter changed the view and not the address bar, and a
copied link reproduced the page you arrived at rather than the one you were looking at. Added
`onSortChange` and `onFilterChange`, both additive; the re-sync they enable is **gated on their
presence**, so the twenty-odd existing grids are provably untouched. 🔴 The re-sync keys off
`defaultSort?.key`/`?.dir` **primitives** — every consumer passes an inline object literal, and an
effect on its identity would fire forever. Offered to every session in session-locks §4.

**The bug the prod verification caught.** Null-last was written as `return 1` inside a comparator
whose caller did `sign * cmp(a, b)` — so `sort=acos:desc` flipped it to null-**first** and the top
of the grid was five rows of "—". 247 of the 521 measured targets have no sales, so this was the
common case. `cmp` now takes the direction and applies the null rule outside it. Found by
`_bid-s0-verify.mts` against production, not by reading the code.

### A defect this found, and the fix that shipped with it

**21 of the first 100 rendered rows had a blank Target cell** — including the 7th-largest spender
on the page. Measured across the account: **256 of 3,154 targets (8.1%) carry an empty
`expressionValue`, holding €608.56 of 30-day spend (18% of the account's total).**

| kind | rows |
|---|---|
| AUTO | 175 |
| PRODUCT_CATEGORY | 32 |
| PRODUCT_AUDIENCE | 28 |
| AUDIENCE | 14 |
| PRODUCT_CATEGORY_AUDIENCE | 7 |

None of them *has* a text expression, because none of them *is* one: an auto-targeting row is
identified by its group, exactly as Seller Central names it. A blank there is not cosmetic — that
column is the sort key, the search key, and what S3 will hang the bid curve off.

Fixed by `labelFor(text, kind, match)` **on the server**, so the grid, the CSV, the search and every
later section say the same words: `SEARCH_CLOSE_MATCH` → *Close match*, `PRODUCT_SUBSTITUTES` →
*Substitutes*, and so on. Where the match says nothing (`UNKNOWN`, 53 rows) it falls back to the
kind rather than guessing a group. The row keeps `text` raw alongside `label` + `derived`, the cell
renders derived names in italic at a lighter weight — **never a different colour, because colour in
that column already means "is a link"** — and the CSV carries a `Target name derived` column.

Found by looking at the deployed page. It would not have shown up in any test I wrote.

### A second defect the same pass found: two sentences counting a different set from the grid

With `?kind=AUTO` applied, the floor line read **"24 targets sit at €0.05 or below"** directly
beneath a census cell reading **2,944**. Both numbers were right and they counted different things:
the census counts the **scope**, the facets exclude their own dimension but apply every other
filter, so they count **the rows on screen**. Stacked one under the other, they read as one set, and
the smaller number looks like an account-wide fact.

Both sentences now carry the denominator they were computed against — *"24 of the 123 targets in
this view"*. A guard has to share the denominator of the value it guards.

## 7 · What the next sections need to know

1. **`BidSections.tsx` is the seam.** Every section is one file and one line there; nobody
   restructures `BidClient`. `BidSlotProps` already carries scope, view, rows, campaigns, loading,
   `push`, `reload`, the three reserved params and the refresh state.
2. **`NO_WRITE_ACTIONS` is passed explicitly, not omitted.** The first section that writes replaces
   that object; it does not quietly stop passing it.
3. **The first column is not a link in the targets view** and the shared grid paints it blue at
   `(0,3,1)` with `cursor: pointer` and a hover underline. All three are undone at `(0,3,2)` on
   `span.t`, at the END of the stylesheet, so the campaigns view — where the cell really is an `<a>`
   — keeps its blue. **S3 deletes those two rules** when the target becomes a real link into the bid
   curve. KT.1 fixed the colour and left the pointer and the underline; do not repeat that.
4. **Facets exclude their own dimension.** Any chip a later section adds must do the same, or it
   will advertise a count it cannot deliver.
5. **Every clickable census cell must reproduce its own number.** Two of S0's five carry no click
   because no filter would return them; that is the correct answer, not a gap.
6. **`bandOf` is total and contiguous over the integers**, pinned by a test. Moving a boundary
   changes what the floor chip means — 5¢ is `BID_FLOOR_CENTS` in the handlers and `FLOOR_CENTS` in
   the optimiser, so `0-5` is exactly the population both consider already at the floor.
7. **The campaign roll-up recomputes ACoS and CPC from summed spend and sales**, never from a mean
   of per-target ratios.
8. **S5 must not create `AdBidPolicy` before reading §2.2.** `minBidCents` is set on **0 of 217**
   campaigns holding a target and `maxBidCents` on **81**.
